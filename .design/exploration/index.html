Udgam capture app and certificate: final design for approval

Each direction is a working phone prototype of the same seven screens, plus the public certificate a buyer reaches from the QR code. They share the same copy, data and rules. They differ in how they make a farmer feel sure the record is theirs and is right.

On a phone

Open an app and tap through: record, photo, weight, checking, result. The Screens button jumps to any screen.

On a laptop

Each app opens as a storyboard of all seven screens side by side. Narrow the window below 1000 px to use it as a phone.

What to decide

Pick a direction, or mix parts, such as B's map with C's Kannada labels. The recommendation is at the end.

Estate Record Book

Option A
Idea
The app feels like the picking book farmers and FPO staff already keep. Every verified picking is pressed with a seal, as if sealed into your own book.
Where it is bold
The circular seal on the verdict screen and on each entry.
Look
Paper-toned page, blue-black ink, hairline rules instead of boxes, Noto Serif for dates and kilos, cherry red for the one action.
Feels like
Pride and permanence. Quiet and literate.

Checked on the rendered pages

  • Measured at 320, 375, 768 and 1440 px across 40 page states: no sideways scroll, no tap target under 24 px, no script errors. Its builder also exercised the whole flow and the failure state.
  • Design detector: the paper background reads as the reflex cream look. It also found a small uppercase label above the certificate heading and wide letter-spacing on some text. All fixable in the final.

Your Plot, Proven

Option B
Idea
The farmer's own plot outline, with their location dot inside it, is on every screen. The main fraud check, "were you really in your plot?", becomes something they can see.
Where it is bold
The illustrated plot map: laterite-red fence line, contour lines, shade trees.
Look
Mist-green page, deep canopy-green text, Anek (one family for English and Kannada), leaf green for the action.
Feels like
Recognition: "that is my land." Calm and precise.

One Thing at a Time

Option C
Idea
A short conversation, one question per screen, the way the FPO field officer would ask. Kannada on top, English beneath, and a Hear this button on every question.
Where it is bold
Full-screen colour for the result: green, turmeric or clay, with one symbol and one sentence.
Look
Warm white page, near-black text, Baloo Tamma 2 (made for Kannada and English together), cherry red for the action.
Feels like
Being guided by someone patient. The least reading of the three.

What I need from you

  1. Do you approve this UI/UX direction? The final app, certificate, admin review and link preview above. Approval freezes the look, the screens and the flow; later stages may fix technical and accessibility details but not redesign it.
  2. The cherry. At large sizes it can read as a plum or an apple. Real coffee cherries are small, oval and grow in clusters. I recommend a cluster of three oval cherries on a short branch. Keep it, or change it?
  3. Who holds the phone? The plan has the field agent log in, with the farmer as a record. Should farmers log in themselves? It changes enrolment and the home screen, not the look.

Prototypes use drawn placeholders instead of real farm photos, invented but realistic plot and batch data, and Kannada that still needs review by a native speaker. None of it is production code.
